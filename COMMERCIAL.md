## You do not need a commercial license to

- Read, run, change and share Drafter, or publish your changes, under the AGPL.
- Make a game, tool or service whose complete source you publish under the AGPL.
- Evaluate Drafter, teach with it, or use it for research whose results you
  share under the AGPL or a compatible license.
- Use it for your own purposes without distributing anything. The AGPL's
  source obligation is triggered by distributing the work or by offering it to
  other people over a network, not by using it.

## You need a commercial license to

- Distribute a game that links to Drafter without publishing that game's
  complete corresponding source under the AGPL. This is the common case for a
  commercial game: the engine is the AGPL part, and the game around it is not.
- Offer a game or service built on Drafter to other people over a network
  without offering them its source (AGPL section 13).
- Ship Drafter in a product whose terms are not compatible with the AGPL, or
  where you cannot meet its notices and source offer.

A commercial license covers the game, product or service named in it. It does
not relicense Drafter itself: copies you redistribute still carry the AGPL.
It is a separate agreement, not an exception built into this repository.

## What it costs

Priced per organization rather than per seat or per machine, and quoted for the
product you are shipping. Nothing is charged for the size of your players or
for the number of servers running the game.

## How to ask

Open an issue at <https://github.com/jaman/drafter/issues> with the subject
"Commercial license", or write to the maintainer named in `mix.exs`. Say what
you are building, whether it is distributed or served, and roughly how many
people work on it. You will get terms, not a sales call.

## The dependencies

Drafter depends on packages with their own licenses: `french_curve`,
`elixir_make`, `phoenix_pubsub`, and their transitive dependencies. A
commercial license for Drafter says nothing about them; check each one.
